RV camping in Jefferson County, Washington

Thirteen verified public and private camping options from the Hoh Rain Forest and Hood Canal to Port Townsend, compared by setting, hookups, rig fit, and how you actually get a site.

Reviewed September 9, 2026 · 14 minute read

Jefferson County gives RV travelers thirteen verified options without forcing every trip into the same camping style. You can stay beside the Dosewallips River, reserve private hookups near Hood Canal, use a first-come county site, camp near the Hoh Rain Forest, explore a coastal fort, or park within walking distance of Port Townsend. This guide combines the completed state, county, city, port, private, and tribal inventory passes with the federal campgrounds already reviewed.

How to choose your base

  • Choose Point Hudson for full hookups and the easiest walk into Port Townsend; verify your exact pad length during booking.
  • Choose Cove RV Park for online booking, full hookups, and on-site services along the Brinnon side of Hood Canal.
  • Choose Port Ludlow RV Park for a wooded full-hookup stay near Ludlow Falls and the marina; reservations are handled by phone.
  • Choose Hard Rain when Hoh Rain Forest access matters most and water-and-electric service is enough for your rig.
  • Choose the Fairgrounds for a reservable, service-rich Port Townsend base that can fit selected rigs up to 50 feet.
  • Choose Upper Oak Bay when you want inexpensive electrical service and can arrive early enough for first-come camping.
  • Choose Lake Leland or Quilcene for a small, simple county stay; all county options cap vehicles at 30 feet.
  • Choose Dosewallips when hookups and easy access to both river and Hood Canal environments are your priority.
  • Choose Fort Flagler for a destination campground with beaches, history, and roomier RV options away from town.
  • Choose Fort Worden when restaurants, Port Townsend, and a walkable waterfront setting matter more than seclusion.
  • Choose Seal Rock for a simpler national-forest stay and you are comfortable camping without hookups.

Driving from Seattle

The campground pages use stable no-ferry planning estimates: about one hour and 45 minutes to Port Ludlow, two hours to Quilcene, Lake Leland, and Oak Bay, two hours and 15 minutes to Cove RV Park, two and a half hours to Port Townsend, and four hours to Hard Rain. Google often shows a shorter moving time via Bainbridge or Edmonds, but that number can omit the wait that matters when towing. Always compare the live ferry queue, fare, and sailing schedule before choosing the ferry.

A practical two-stop trip

Pair one Hood Canal or lake campground with one northern base. Dosewallips, Cove, Seal Rock, Quilcene, and Lake Leland cover the southern half of the county. Port Ludlow, Fort Flagler, and the two Oak Bay campgrounds work well around the Hood Canal Bridge, Port Hadlock, and Marrowstone Island. Fort Worden, Point Hudson, and the Fairgrounds cover Port Townsend. Hard Rain is far enough west to treat as its own Hoh Rain Forest stop.
