RV camping in Jefferson County, Washington

Seventeen verified campground records from the Pacific coast and Hoh Rain Forest to Hood Canal and Port Townsend, compared by current status, setting, hookups, rig fit, and how you actually get a site.

Reviewed September 9, 2026 · 18 minute read

Jefferson County gives RV travelers choices across the Pacific coast, Hoh Rain Forest, Hood Canal, and Port Townsend. You can reserve private hookups, use a first-come county or federal site, camp beside the ocean, explore a coastal fort, or choose a primitive Washington DNR river camp. This guide now reflects completed state, federal, DNR, county, city, port, private, and tribal inventory passes. It keeps current closures visible so an unavailable campground is not mistaken for a trip option.

How to choose your base

  • Choose Point Hudson for full hookups and the easiest walk into Port Townsend; verify your exact pad length during booking.
  • Choose Cove RV Park for online booking, full hookups, and on-site services along the Brinnon side of Hood Canal.
  • Choose Port Ludlow RV Park for a wooded full-hookup stay near Ludlow Falls and the marina; reservations are handled by phone.
  • Choose Hard Rain when Hoh Rain Forest access matters most and water-and-electric service is enough for your rig.
  • Choose Hoh Oxbow for the simplest DNR road approach, or Minnie Peterson for a small creekside camp on Upper Hoh Road.
  • Choose Coppermine Bottom or South Fork Hoh only when your rig and supplies are ready for long primitive forest-road access.
  • Choose the Fairgrounds for a reservable, service-rich Port Townsend base that can fit selected rigs up to 50 feet.
  • Choose Upper Oak Bay when you want inexpensive electrical service and can arrive early enough for first-come camping.
  • Choose Lake Leland or Quilcene for a small, simple county stay; all county options cap vehicles at 30 feet.
  • Choose Dosewallips when hookups and easy access to both river and Hood Canal environments are your priority.
  • Choose Fort Flagler for a destination campground with beaches, history, and roomier RV options away from town.
  • Choose Fort Worden when restaurants, Port Townsend, and a walkable waterfront setting matter more than seclusion.
  • Choose Falls View for a small, dry National Forest stop near Quilcene; confirm the open loop if your RV approaches 35 feet.
  • Choose Hoh for the shortest start to the Hoh Rain Forest trails, or Kalaloch for a larger reservable coastal base.
  • Choose South Beach for first-come ocean-bluff camping when you can arrive with all the water you need.
  • Keep Seal Rock as a future option after the Forest Service posts a reopening; the current published RV limit is 21 feet.

Driving from Seattle

The campground pages use stable no-ferry planning estimates: about one hour and 45 minutes to Port Ludlow, two hours to Quilcene, Falls View, Lake Leland, and Oak Bay, two hours and 15 minutes to Cove and Seal Rock, two and a half hours to Port Townsend, a little over three hours to Kalaloch and South Beach, and three hours 45 minutes to four hours 15 minutes for the DNR and Hoh corridor sites. The DNR estimates add towing margin for gravel or primitive access roads. Google often shows a shorter ferry route or moving time that omits the wait that matters when towing.

A practical two-stop trip

Pair one Hood Canal or lake campground with one northern or western base. Dosewallips, Cove, Falls View, Quilcene, and Lake Leland cover the southern half of the county; Seal Rock returns to that group after reopening. Port Ludlow, Fort Flagler, and the two Oak Bay campgrounds work well around the Hood Canal Bridge and Marrowstone Island. Fort Worden, Point Hudson, and the Fairgrounds cover Port Townsend. Treat Hoh and Hard Rain as rain-forest bases, and Kalaloch or South Beach as a separate coastal stop.
