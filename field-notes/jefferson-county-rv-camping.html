RV camping in Jefferson County, Washington

Four useful bases from Hood Canal to Port Townsend, compared by setting, hookups, and rig fit so you can find the stop that matches your trip.

Reviewed September 6, 2026 · 7 minute read

Jefferson County gives RV travelers several very different versions of the Olympic Peninsula without requiring a long move between camps. You can stay beside the Dosewallips River, camp near Hood Canal, explore a military fort on Marrowstone Island, or park close to Port Townsend. The best choice depends less on a single ranking than on the experience and services you want.

Best service mixDosewallips has standard, water-and-electric, and full-hookup ranges.
Best historic settingFort Flagler pairs two campgrounds with batteries, beaches, and broad water views.
Best for Port TownsendFort Worden puts camping, shoreline, and town access together.
Best simple Hood Canal stopSeal Rock offers public shoreline access in an Olympic National Forest campground.

How to choose your base

  • Choose Dosewallips when hookups and easy access to both river and Hood Canal environments are your priority.
  • Choose Fort Flagler for a destination campground with beaches, history, and roomier RV options away from town.
  • Choose Fort Worden when restaurants, Port Townsend, and a walkable waterfront setting matter more than seclusion.
  • Choose Seal Rock for a simpler national-forest stay and you are comfortable camping without hookups.

A practical two-stop trip

For a first Jefferson County trip, pair one Hood Canal campground with one northern fort. Dosewallips and Seal Rock are close enough to compare as bases rather than treating both as mandatory stops. Fort Flagler and Fort Worden offer different sides of the same regional story: one feels like an island destination, while the other connects directly to Port Townsend. Linking two contrasting stays creates a more varied trip and gives you a backup if your first campground is full.
