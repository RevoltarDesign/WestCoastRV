RV camping in Jefferson County, Washington

Ten verified public camping options from Hood Canal to Port Townsend, compared by setting, hookups, rig fit, and how you actually get a site.

Reviewed September 9, 2026 · 11 minute read

Jefferson County gives RV travelers ten distinct public options without a long move between camps. You can stay beside the Dosewallips River, camp near Hood Canal, use a first-come county site, explore a coastal fort, or reserve full hookups in Port Townsend. This guide includes every campground resolved in the county, city, port, state-park, and existing federal pass completed so far.

Ten campgrounds worth comparing

Each page uses the same fields for rig length, hookups, facilities, access, and booking. County camps are first-come and limited to 30-foot vehicles. Port Townsend's two local options take online reservations, while the state and federal choices follow their own operator systems.

How to choose your base

  • Choose Point Hudson for full hookups and the easiest walk into Port Townsend; verify your exact pad length during booking.
  • Choose the Fairgrounds for a reservable, service-rich Port Townsend base that can fit selected rigs up to 50 feet.
  • Choose Upper Oak Bay when you want inexpensive electrical service and can arrive early enough for first-come camping.
  • Choose Lake Leland or Quilcene for a small, simple county stay; all county options cap vehicles at 30 feet.
  • Choose Dosewallips when hookups and easy access to both river and Hood Canal environments are your priority.
  • Choose Fort Flagler for a destination campground with beaches, history, and roomier RV options away from town.
  • Choose Fort Worden when restaurants, Port Townsend, and a walkable waterfront setting matter more than seclusion.
  • Choose Seal Rock for a simpler national-forest stay and you are comfortable camping without hookups.

Driving from Seattle

The campground pages use a stable no-ferry route through Tacoma Narrows for their planning estimates: about two hours to Quilcene, Lake Leland, and Oak Bay, and about two and a half hours to Port Townsend. Google often shows a shorter moving time via Bainbridge or Edmonds, but that number can omit the wait that matters when towing. Always compare the live ferry queue, fare, and sailing schedule before choosing the ferry.

A practical two-stop trip

Pair one Hood Canal or lake campground with one northern base. Dosewallips, Seal Rock, Quilcene, and Lake Leland cover the southern half of the county. Fort Flagler and the two Oak Bay campgrounds work well around Port Hadlock and Marrowstone Island. Fort Worden, Point Hudson, and the Fairgrounds let you choose between a destination park, a walkable marina, and a practical reservable base in Port Townsend.
